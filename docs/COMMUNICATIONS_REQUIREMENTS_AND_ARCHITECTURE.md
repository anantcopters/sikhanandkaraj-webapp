# SikhanandKaraj Communications Requirements and Architecture

## 1. Document purpose

This document defines the product requirements, business rules, user experience, channel strategy, implemented email baseline and target architecture for communications generated by SikhanandKaraj.

It is intended to be the source requirement for development of:

- Email
- In-app/System Notifications
- SMS
- WhatsApp messaging
- Member communication preferences
- Administrative communication tooling
- Communication audit and delivery monitoring
- Template preview and QA

This document describes required product behaviour and architecture boundaries. Implementation-specific details are recorded where they have become approved project architecture and must be preserved by later phases.

**Current implementation branch:** `email_service`

# 2. Product context

SikhanandKaraj is a matrimonial platform. Communication behaviour must therefore be designed differently from a generic social network or marketing platform.

A matrimonial communication may disclose that:

- a person has a matrimonial profile;
- somebody expressed matrimonial Interest;
- an Interest was accepted or declined;
- somebody interacted with a profile;
- a member submitted Aadhaar or Video Introduction verification;
- a profile or media item was moderated;
- a membership was purchased or is expiring;
- a member created or received a response to a support request.

These facts can be privacy-sensitive, particularly when notifications appear on a shared mobile phone, family email account, lock screen, SMS inbox or WhatsApp account.

The platform must therefore optimize for:

1. Timely communication of important matrimonial events.
2. Privacy and member safety.
3. Trustworthy transactional communication.
4. Low communication fatigue.
5. Appropriate Free-to-Paid conversion without harassment.
6. Reliable delivery and operational visibility.
7. Consistent content across channels.
8. Long-term maintainability as communication volume grows.

# 3. Current-state baseline

The `email_service` implementation now provides a reusable email foundation rather than isolated feature-specific mail sending.

## 3.1 Implemented email foundation

The application currently includes:

- Central `EmailRegistry` definitions with deterministic preview data.
- `MemberEmailService` as the application boundary for normal member email communication.
- `MemberEmailRecipientService` for authoritative member email eligibility.
- `AdminEmailService` for administrative email communication.
- Durable `email_queue` persistence.
- Worker-based asynchronous email delivery.
- Bounded retry handling and stale-job recovery.
- Common branded email layout and reusable components.
- Email Preview Centre using the production renderer.
- Test-send support through the real queue/worker/provider pipeline.
- Shared presentation support for member-facing dates and Indian Rupee formatting.

## 3.2 Implemented email events

The standardized email architecture currently covers:

- Member email verification/replacement verification.
- Administrator invitation.
- Interest received.
- Interest accepted.
- Interest declined.
- Profile photo rejected.
- Aadhaar approved.
- Aadhaar rejected.
- Video Introduction approved.
- Video Introduction rejected.
- Video Introduction resubmission requested.
- Support request received.
- Support request resolved.
- Membership activated/payment confirmation.
- Membership expiring soon reminder.
- Membership expired.

Photo approval intentionally does not currently generate email because it does not require member action and would add unnecessary communication volume.

High-volume events such as Profile Viewed and Shortlisted are intentionally deferred until the preference/digest phase rather than being introduced as immediate emails.

## 3.3 Existing in-app/system notification baseline

The application also has an in-app member notification mechanism covering events including:

- Interest received.
- Interest accepted.
- Interest declined/rejected.
- Profile photo rejection.
- Video Introduction approved.
- Video Introduction rejected.
- Video Introduction resubmission requested.
- System/member operational messages.

The future architecture must continue extending these foundations rather than create unrelated delivery mechanisms for each feature.

# 4. Product principles

## 4.1 Business events, not channels, are the source of communication

Feature workflows should communicate that a business event occurred, for example:

- `INTEREST_RECEIVED`
- `INTEREST_ACCEPTED`
- `VIDEO_REJECTED`
- `MEMBER_MEMBERSHIP_EXPIRING_SOON`

The communication subsystem determines which channels should be used.

A feature should not independently invent an email, SMS and WhatsApp message.

## 4.2 Server-side facts are authoritative

Communication content must be generated from authoritative application state. Client-supplied names, membership types, verification statuses, URLs or relationship states must not be trusted as communication facts.

Commercial membership emails must use immutable purchased membership/payment snapshots where available rather than current plan-master values that may have changed after purchase.

## 4.3 Privacy before engagement

The objective is not to maximize notifications. The objective is to deliver the minimum useful information through the appropriate channel.

## 4.4 No channel may bypass product authorization

A communication must never expose profile information that the recipient is not permitted to see inside the application.

A notification link may invite the recipient to view a profile, but opening that link must still execute normal current authorization rules.

## 4.5 Communications must remain useful after state changes

An email may be opened hours or days after it was generated. The message may say that an event occurred, but its CTA must not imply that access is guaranteed.

For example, a profile may subsequently become hidden, blocked or unavailable.

## 4.6 Communication should drive members back to SikhanandKaraj

Except where technically required for verification/authentication, communication should generally summarize the event and direct the member to the authenticated SikhanandKaraj experience rather than exposing sensitive details directly in external channels.

## 4.7 Optional communication must not roll back the business event

For normal member communication, the authoritative matrimonial, moderation, support, payment or membership state is committed first. Email is then queued as a downstream best-effort communication.

Failure to resolve a verified email or queue an optional email must not undo a successfully committed business event.

Security workflows such as email verification remain explicit exceptions where the communication itself is part of completing the security/verification transaction.

# 5. Channel model

SikhanandKaraj should support four communication channels.

| Channel | Primary role | Sensitive detail level | Delivery expectation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| In-app/System | Rich member activity and operational messages | Highest permitted level | Available inside authenticated application |
| Email | Transactional, important activity, membership and controlled engagement | Moderate | Asynchronous, durable |
| SMS | OTP/security/urgent short alerts | Minimal | Near real-time, cost-controlled |
| WhatsApp | Opted-in high-value transactional/activity communication | Minimal to moderate | Near real-time, provider/template controlled |

WhatsApp is a future-capable channel and must be represented in the architecture even if not enabled initially.

# 6. Communication categories

Every communication definition must belong to a category.

| Category | Examples | Member opt-out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SECURITY | OTP, password/security event | No where essential |
| VERIFICATION | Email verification, verification action required | No where essential |
| TRANSACTIONAL | Payment, membership activation | No |
| MATRIMONIAL_ACTIVITY | Interest received/accepted/declined | Configurable subject to product rules |
| MODERATION | Photo/Aadhaar/Video decision | No for important decisions |
| MEMBERSHIP | Activation, expiry, expiry reminder, quota/plan information | Essential lifecycle events no; future promotional reminders configurable |
| SUPPORT | Ticket acknowledgement/resolution | No for active support case |
| ENGAGEMENT | New matches, incomplete profile reminder | Yes |
| MARKETING | Offers, campaigns, announcements | Yes; explicit consent required where applicable |

Transactional and marketing consent must never be treated as the same thing.

# 8. Member email eligibility and Free vs Paid implications

## 8.1 Verified primary email is a prerequisite

For normal member email communication, the recipient must have a current primary EMAIL contact with `is_verified = true`.

Recipient resolution must be centralized through the member email recipient boundary rather than repeated inside feature services.

If no verified primary email exists:

- the email is skipped silently;
- the originating business event remains successful;
- existing in-app/status behaviour remains authoritative;
- the system must not fall back to an unverified or arbitrary email address.

During replacement-email verification, the existing verified primary email remains the normal communication target until the replacement address is successfully verified and promoted.

### Verification exception

Email Verification itself must be allowed to send to an unverified destination because verification is the purpose of that email. It therefore bypasses the normal verified-primary-email prerequisite while retaining token expiry, replay protection and the normal durable queue/worker pipeline.

## 8.2 Communication must not become a paid-feature bypass

Free members may receive notifications about legitimate activity, but the communication must not reveal paid-only profile information.

Example:

> You received a new Interest on SikhanandKaraj.

The external message should not expose information that requires paid Full Profile access.

When the member follows the CTA, normal Free/Paid authorization applies.

## 8.3 Conversion opportunities

Communications may be used for contextual conversion when the event is genuine.

Recommended pattern:

1. Communicate the real event.
2. Bring the member into the application.
3. Show the appropriate account capability and upgrade explanation inside the authenticated UI.

Avoid turning transactional emails into advertisements.

## 8.4 Paid members

Paid membership may affect what the member can do after opening a notification, but should not automatically result in substantially higher external communication frequency.

Paid status is an entitlement, not consent to more marketing.

## 8.5 Membership expiry policy

The currently approved email cadence is deliberately simple:

1. One `MEMBER_MEMBERSHIP_EXPIRING_SOON` email exactly **3 member-facing calendar days before expiry**.
2. One `MEMBER_MEMBERSHIP_EXPIRED` email after the membership has actually expired and lifecycle housekeeping transitions the persisted row from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`.

The reminder must not use a rolling 72-hour interpretation. It selects the calendar day three days ahead in the configured member-facing timezone and converts that calendar-day window to UTC for querying authoritative membership timestamps.

The current display timezone defaults to `Asia/Kolkata` and remains configuration-driven.

The reminder is a lifecycle/renewal communication, not a payment receipt. It may show the immutable purchased plan name and validity date, but must not invent a payment or expose provider identifiers.

No additional 7-day/1-day reminder is approved at this stage. Future cadence expansion belongs behind the Phase 3 preference/policy layer.

# 9. Matrimonial privacy requirements

## 9.1 External channel minimization

Email/SMS/WhatsApp should not unnecessarily include:

- Full phone numbers of another member.
- Email addresses of another member.
- Aadhaar information, including Aadhaar name or DOB captured during verification.
- Aadhaar document links.
- Private gallery images.
- Video Introduction media URLs.
- Full profile data.
- Family contact information.
- Sensitive moderation/internal notes.
- Payment-provider raw responses or provider identifiers.

## 9.2 Female-member privacy

Existing name masking and Male -> Female access rules must continue to apply to communications.

Communication generation must not become an alternate path for revealing a female member's full identity or protected profile information.

Current Interest emails intentionally communicate the event without exposing the other member's identity.

## 9.3 Shared devices

SMS and WhatsApp text may appear on a lock screen. Subjects and message previews must therefore remain discreet.

Prefer:

> You have a new Interest on SikhanandKaraj.

rather than externally exposing detailed personal information.

## 9.4 URLs

External messages should use application-controlled HTTPS URLs.

URLs must not embed sensitive PII in query strings.

Authentication/authorization must be re-evaluated when the URL is opened.

Single-use security/verification tokens must have expiry and replay protection.

# 10. Trust and safety requirements

Communications must reinforce that SikhanandKaraj is a moderated matrimonial platform rather than an unrestricted social network.

Important moderation decisions should communicate:

- What action occurred.
- Whether member action is required.
- A member-safe reason where appropriate.
- A direct route to the relevant account area.

Internal moderation notes must never automatically become member-visible reasons.

For support resolution, only the explicitly member-facing response note may be copied to email. Internal administrator identity/notes and the member's original potentially sensitive support message must not be unnecessarily copied into external email.

Templates should not encourage members to move conversations outside the platform prematurely.

No automated communication should reveal who reported a profile, who blocked a profile, or internal fraud/risk signals.

# 11. Abuse and misuse scenarios

## 11.1 Notification spam through repeated actions

A malicious member could repeatedly trigger Interest, shortlist or other actions to cause external notifications.

Requirements:

- Business workflows must prevent invalid repeated state transitions.
- Communication generation should be idempotent where appropriate.
- High-volume activity should be rate-limited, deduplicated or digested.

## 11.2 Enumeration

Communication APIs and preview tools must not allow arbitrary member IDs/emails/mobile numbers to be used to discover accounts.

## 11.3 External recipient manipulation

Recipients must be resolved from authoritative verified contact records. A request must not be able to supply an arbitrary destination for a real member event.

An explicitly administrative `Send Test` facility is an exception and must be permission-controlled and clearly marked as test communication.

## 11.4 Link forwarding

Normal authenticated links remain subject to login and authorization.

Security tokens must be single-purpose, expiring and preferably single-use.

## 11.5 Template injection

Dynamic content must be safely escaped/encoded for the destination channel.

Member-provided free text must not be allowed to inject HTML or alter message structure.

## 11.6 WhatsApp/SMS cost abuse

SMS and WhatsApp have direct per-message/provider cost implications.

Requirements:

- Rate limits.
- Retry limits.
- Provider failure handling.
- Event deduplication.
- Administrative visibility into volume.
- Marketing campaigns must never accidentally use transactional triggers.

# 12. Target communication architecture

## 12.1 Conceptual flow

```text
Business Workflow / Domain Event
              |
              v
     Communication Service
              |
      Communication Policy
              |
    +---------+---------+----------+
    |                   |          |
    v                   v          v
In-App/System          Email      SMS       WhatsApp
Notification            |          |           |
Service                  v          v           v
                    Email Queue  SMS Queue  WhatsApp Queue
                         |          |           |
                         v          v           v
                     Worker      Worker      Worker
                         |          |           |
                         v          v           v
                     Provider    Provider    Provider
```

## 12.2 Implemented email flow

Normal member email currently follows this concrete boundary:

```text
Authoritative business workflow
          |
          | commit business state / in-app state
          v
   MemberEmailService
          |
          v
MemberEmailRecipientService
          |
          | verified primary EMAIL?
      +---+---+
      |       |
      No      Yes
      |       |
    Skip      v
          EmailRegistry
              |
              v
         EmailQueueService
              |
              v
           Worker
              |
              v
          Mail provider
```

Email Verification is the intentional exception because its destination is not yet verified.

## 12.3 Architecture principle

Feature/domain services should communicate the business event and required business context. They should not contain provider-specific delivery logic.

## 12.4 Email subsystem responsibilities

The implemented/target email subsystem has distinct responsibilities:

```text
Email
  - Email definitions/registry
  - Member email application service
  - Verified member recipient resolver
  - Admin email application service
  - Common layout/components
  - Template renderer
  - Preview/test support
  - Durable queue
  - Worker
  - Mail provider adapter/service
```

Existing queue/worker delivery must be reused.

## 12.5 Channel independence

Failure of one optional channel must not normally roll back the underlying matrimonial business event.

Example:

An Interest successfully stored in the database must not disappear merely because email or future WhatsApp delivery is unavailable.

For communications that are part of a security transaction, the relevant workflow may have stricter delivery requirements.

## 12.6 Event identity and idempotency

Where duplicate delivery would be harmful, a communication should be identifiable by:

- Event type.
- Recipient.
- Business entity/reference.
- Event occurrence/version where necessary.

The durable email queue already carries `reference_type` and `reference_id` and these should be used consistently for business traceability and idempotency where appropriate.

The membership expiry reminder currently uses a reminder-specific reference type plus purchased membership ID so a normal rerun of the scheduled reminder process does not intentionally create another reminder for the same membership.

A queue row in `PENDING`, `PROCESSING`, `SENT` or `FAILED` counts as an already-generated reminder. A failed delivery remains the queue retry mechanism's responsibility; the next morning's cron must not manufacture a fresh duplicate communication.

Current reminder deduplication is operational idempotency for the single scheduler/manual rerun model. Concurrency-grade uniqueness across genuinely simultaneous producers should be enforced by the future central communication idempotency/outbox layer rather than by adding communication state to the membership table.

## 12.7 Transactional outbox direction

The current best-effort post-commit email model is appropriate for the present email phase. Before broad SMS/WhatsApp/high-volume communication, a transactional outbox should be considered so a committed business event cannot be permanently separated from required downstream communication by a process crash between commit and queue insertion.

The outbox must remain a communication/integration concern and must not contaminate matrimonial authorization tables with channel-specific state.

# 13. Communication definition/registry requirement

A centralized definition should exist for every supported communication event.

Each definition should specify conceptually:

- Stable event identifier.
- Category.
- Default subject/title.
- Available channels.
- Required channels.
- Template identifiers.
- Priority.
- Default retry policy.
- Preference behaviour.
- Preview/sample data.
- Reference entity type.
- Whether content is privacy-sensitive.
- Whether the event may be digested.

This registry becomes the source of truth for runtime delivery, preview tooling, documentation and QA.

Email subject strings and template paths should not be scattered throughout unrelated feature services.

Current membership definitions include:

- `MEMBER_MEMBERSHIP_ACTIVATED`
- `MEMBER_MEMBERSHIP_EXPIRING_SOON`
- `MEMBER_MEMBERSHIP_EXPIRED`

Current support definitions include:

- `MEMBER_SUPPORT_REQUEST_RECEIVED`
- `MEMBER_SUPPORT_REQUEST_RESOLVED`

# 14. Email design system

## 14.1 Common layout

All SikhanandKaraj member emails should use the common branded layout containing:

- SikhanandKaraj approved logo/identity.
- Inter typography from the project's local font assets where supported by the email client.
- Approved brand colours including purple `#310a57` and red `#ce102c`.
- Header treatment using the approved light background `#f9e9e7`.
- Consistent content width.
- Mobile-responsive structure.
- Typography hierarchy.
- Standard CTA button.
- Standard informational/warning blocks.
- Footer.
- Support route/contact information where appropriate.
- Legal/preferences/unsubscribe links where required by category.

The official phone/contact value must come from project configuration/data and must not be invented inside a template.

## 14.2 Reusable components

Email templates should reuse standard components such as:

- Header/layout.
- Footer.
- Primary CTA.
- Secondary CTA/text link.
- Information box.
- Warning/action-required box.
- Membership summary.
- Privacy note.

The membership activation, expiring-soon and expired emails reuse the same `MembershipActivity` presentation rather than introducing separate near-identical templates.

## 14.3 Shared presentation helpers

Common formatting must remain outside individual templates/services where the project already provides a shared presentation boundary.

Current examples:

- `CurrencyDisplay::formatIndianRupees()` for integer-paise values.
- `DateDisplay::formatUtcDate()` for UTC-to-member-facing date presentation.

Email-specific copies of these formatters must not be introduced.

## 14.4 Email content rules

Every email should answer, in order:

1. Why did I receive this?
2. What happened?
3. Do I need to do anything?
4. What is the safe next action?

Avoid excessive promotional sections in transactional messages.

The preview should show:

- Category.
- Subject.
- Sample recipient context.
- Actual rendered template.

Preview and test-send tooling must not be anonymously accessible.

- Development: available to authorized development users.

# 16. In-app/System Notification UX

## 16.1 Notification centre

The authenticated notification centre should remain the richest communication channel.

A notification should contain:

- Type/icon.
- Short title.
- Concise message.
- Relative/absolute time as appropriate.
- Read/unread state.
- Safe target route.

## 16.2 Read behaviour

Opening a notification may mark it read, but failure to access the target must not reveal hidden information.

## 16.3 Deleted/unavailable targets

Old notifications may point to content that is no longer available. The user should receive a neutral application message rather than an error revealing moderation/block status.

## 16.4 Notification retention

Retention should be configurable. Security/audit records are separate from the member-visible notification history.

# 17. SMS requirements

SMS should be deliberately narrow because of cost, privacy and spam perception.

Recommended primary use:

- OTP/mobile verification.
- Critical account recovery/security events.
- Selected payment/membership transactional alerts.
- Selected urgent reminders only if product evidence supports them.

SMS should not be the default channel for:

- Profile views.
- Shortlists.
- Routine matches.
- Routine moderation approvals.
- Marketing without explicit consent.

SMS content must be short, privacy-safe and must not expose protected member data.

# 18. WhatsApp requirements

## 18.1 Product position

WhatsApp should be treated as an optional high-engagement channel, not as a replacement for the SikhanandKaraj application.

The architecture must allow WhatsApp to be introduced without modifying every matrimonial feature.

## 18.2 Consent

WhatsApp messaging requires a separately manageable opt-in state where required by provider/policy/regulation.

A verified mobile number does not automatically mean the member consented to all WhatsApp communication.

## 18.3 Recommended initial WhatsApp events

When introduced, begin with a small set:

- Interest received.
- Interest accepted.
- Verification action required.
- Video/Aadhaar resubmission required.
- Membership activation.
- Membership expiry reminder.
- Support update.

Do not initially enable profile-view, shortlist or high-volume match notifications.

## 18.4 Content

WhatsApp messages should normally contain:

- SikhanandKaraj identity.
- Short event summary.
- Safe CTA/deep link.
- No protected contact information.
- No private media attachments.

## 18.5 Templates/provider constraints

The WhatsApp subsystem must account for provider-approved templates, template versions, language variants and delivery status.

Business workflows must not depend on provider template names.

## 18.6 Conversation model

This requirement covers outbound platform notifications. It does not automatically authorize open-ended member-to-member WhatsApp communication through SikhanandKaraj.

Any future two-way WhatsApp support requires a separate product/safety review.

# 20. Important user journeys

## Journey A - Interest received

1. Member A sends a valid Interest to Member B.
2. Interest transaction succeeds.
3. Existing authoritative in-app notification is persisted.
4. After successful business commit, member email communication is attempted.
5. Member B's verified primary email is resolved centrally.
6. If no verified primary email exists, email is skipped without undoing the Interest.
7. If eligible, the privacy-safe Interest email enters the durable queue.
8. Member B follows the CTA.
9. Application authenticates Member B and evaluates current authorization/state.
10. Member B accepts or declines through the application.

External channels must not perform the acceptance merely by opening the message and must not disclose the other member's protected identity.

## Journey B - Interest accepted/declined

1. Recipient changes the Interest state authoritatively.
2. Sender receives the existing in-app notification.
3. After commit, the corresponding accepted/declined email is attempted for the sender's verified primary email.
4. CTA opens the relevant SikhanandKaraj Interest area.
5. Current membership/profile authorization still applies.

## Journey C - Verification/moderation decision

1. Admin makes the authoritative photo/Aadhaar/Video decision.
2. Decision/history and existing in-app notification/status are persisted as applicable.
3. After commit, a member email is attempted for the verified primary email.
4. Email contains only member-safe status/reason information.
5. CTA opens the authenticated relevant Account Settings/profile area.

Aadhaar data/documents and Video Introduction media URLs are never placed in the external email.

## Journey D - Support request

1. Member creates a valid Contact Us request.
2. `SAKSUPP-<6 digits>` public reference is persisted.
3. After successful creation, an acknowledgement email is attempted.
4. The email includes the public reference but does not copy the member's original support message.
5. Admin later resolves the request with a member-facing response note.
6. After the resolution commit, the resolved email may include that member-facing note.
7. Internal admin identity/notes are not exposed.

## Journey E - Membership activation

1. Payment processing and membership activation succeed authoritatively.
2. Membership/payment snapshots are persisted.
3. After commit, the activation/payment-confirmation email is attempted for the verified primary email.
4. The email uses immutable purchased plan/payment values and SikhanandKaraj's own transaction reference.
5. Raw provider responses/provider identifiers are not exposed.
6. CTA opens the authenticated membership area.

A repeated already-processed provider callback must not intentionally create another activation email.

## Journey F - Membership expiry reminder

1. A dedicated scheduled CLI process runs once each morning.
2. It calculates the member-facing calendar day exactly three days ahead using configured display timezone.
3. The local calendar-day boundaries are converted to UTC.
4. It queries `ACTIVE` memberships whose authoritative `expires_at` falls inside that UTC window.
5. For each membership, reminder idempotency is checked using the reminder-specific queue reference and membership ID.
6. If already generated, it is skipped.
7. If not generated, normal verified-primary-email eligibility is applied.
8. Eligible reminder enters the existing durable email queue and uses the existing membership email UI.
9. CTA opens membership plans.

The scheduled reminder process only queues email. The existing email worker remains responsible for delivery/retry.

## Journey G - Membership expiry

1. Runtime authorization independently stops paid entitlement when `expires_at` is reached; authorization never waits for cron.
2. Membership lifecycle housekeeping identifies overdue `ACTIVE` rows.
3. Persisted lifecycle status transitions to `EXPIRED`.
4. Only after the lifecycle transition is the membership-expired email attempted.
5. Email failure does not restore paid membership or roll back lifecycle state.

Recommended operations are:

- lifecycle expiry processing frequently, for example every 15 minutes;
- expiry reminder processing once each morning.

Cron clock configuration is an infrastructure concern. The reminder's selected membership date is not dependent on server timezone because the service explicitly uses the configured member-facing timezone and converts the target day to UTC.

## Journey H - Email template QA

1. Authorized QA user opens Email Preview Centre.
2. Selects a registered definition.
3. Reviews the actual rendered desktop/mobile output using deterministic sample data.
4. Selects Send Test.
5. Enters approved test destination.
6. Test message enters normal email queue.
7. Worker sends it through the configured provider.
8. QA verifies client rendering as necessary.

# 21. Edge cases

The implementation/future architecture must explicitly handle:

1. Recipient deletes/deactivates account before queued message sends.
2. Recipient changes email/mobile while message is queued.
3. Email/mobile becomes unverified.
4. Normal member has no verified primary email when an event occurs.
5. Replacement email is pending while old verified primary email remains active.
6. Target profile becomes hidden after event creation.
7. Either member blocks the other after event creation.
8. Interest state changes again before old message is opened.
9. Membership expires before CTA is opened.
10. Same business action is submitted twice.
11. Membership reminder cron is manually rerun on the same day.
12. Membership reminder queue row has permanently failed delivery.
13. Two reminder processes are accidentally started simultaneously.
14. Queue worker retries after provider timeout where provider may actually have accepted the first request.
15. Provider returns permanent invalid-recipient error.
16. WhatsApp template is disabled/rejected by provider.
17. SMS/WhatsApp provider outage.
18. Member revokes marketing/WhatsApp consent while messages are queued.
19. Member changes communication preferences while messages are queued.
20. Long member names/reasons cause layout overflow.
21. Member-controlled text contains HTML/script/URLs.
22. Template variable is missing.
23. Deep link target no longer exists.
24. Locale/language template is unavailable.
25. Test-send feature is accidentally pointed at a real member address.
26. Payment provider retries an already-processed successful callback.
27. Lifecycle expiry and membership replacement/cancellation occur concurrently.

For queued non-essential communications, current consent/preference should preferably be rechecked sufficiently close to dispatch where practical.

# 22. Admin and operational requirements

## 22.1 Communication operations

Authorized administration should eventually provide visibility into:

- Queued.
- Processing.
- Sent/accepted by provider.
- Failed.
- Retry pending.
- Delivery/bounce status where provider supports it.
- Channel.
- Event type.
- Recipient reference without unnecessarily exposing full contact data.
- Created/sent timestamps.
- Failure reason safe for admin use.

## 22.2 Retry

Automatic retry must be bounded.

The current email queue uses bounded attempts and owns delivery retry. Scheduled business jobs must not create fresh duplicate emails merely because an earlier queue row reached `FAILED`.

Manual retry, if provided, must be permission-controlled and audited.

## 22.3 Scheduled membership jobs

Membership communication currently has two operational concerns:

1. Lifecycle expiry processing, which synchronizes persisted `ACTIVE -> EXPIRED` status and then attempts the expired email.
2. Morning expiry-reminder processing, which queues the one-time three-day reminder.

Both must use CLI-only entry points and the existing application service container. Neither job may contain direct provider/SMTP logic.

The email worker remains a separate process responsible for actual sending.

## 22.4 Template management

Initial implementation keeps version-controlled templates in the application repository.

Do not begin with unrestricted database-editable email/WhatsApp HTML because this increases security, QA and operational risk.

A future CMS/template editor can be considered separately if operational demand justifies it.

## 22.5 Audit

Administrative test sends, manual retries, template/configuration changes and bulk campaigns should be auditable.

## 22.6 Bulk communication

Bulk marketing/broadcast communication is outside the initial transactional architecture and should require a dedicated feature with:

- Audience definition.
- Consent filtering.
- Preview.
- Approval.
- Scheduling.
- Rate limiting.
- Cancellation.
- Delivery reporting.

# 30. Final product recommendation

SikhanandKaraj should continue building a unified communication platform around **business events and channel policy**, rather than adding email, SMS and WhatsApp independently inside each feature.

The Email foundation and the approved Phase 2 transactional/member email set are now established. The next architectural priority should therefore be Phase 3: unified communication policy, preferences, digesting, stronger central idempotency/outbox semantics where needed, administration and delivery metrics.

In-app notifications should remain the richest and safest matrimonial activity channel. Email should handle important transactional/activity communication and controlled engagement. SMS should remain deliberately narrow. WhatsApp should be introduced later as an explicit-consent, template-controlled high-value channel.

Most importantly, communications must never become an alternate route around SikhanandKaraj's privacy, moderation or Free/Paid authorization rules. External channels should tell the member that something relevant happened and safely bring them back into the authenticated application, where current policy remains authoritative.
